# CALIBRO
# Concept & Design Document of basically Boynetta

*for inquiries: boynetta.inquiries@icloud.com*

*Working title. Everything here is a starting point, not a constraint, except his sexuality and the general vibe*

-----

## THE PITCH

*In four lines:*

Europe does Europe through Japanese eyes.
The male hero is done through the eyes of gay men.
Cesare stands beside Bayonetta, not beneath her.
The crossover doesn’t need to happen. The mirror image is enough.

-----

## WHAT THIS GAME IS

A character action game in the tradition of Bayonetta — maximalist, committed, completely sincere about its own absurdity. A European game lovingly channeling Japanese game design with complete sincerity and without irony. The cultural joke runs in both directions and everyone is eventually in on it.

The social commentary is embedded in the premise without ever being stated aloud. The game is excellent first. Everything else follows from that.

A gay man in a perfect white suit and his 200-year-old French companion dismantle a vampire mafia in a Mediterranean city. To Europop. The game does not find this unusual.

**Cesare is Luka if Luka was Cereza.**
This is his game.

-----

## REFERENCE POINTS

**Tone:** Bayonetta — complete sincerity about its own absurdity
**Combat feel:** Devil May Cry — stylish, rhythmic, expressive, arrives places dramatically
**Female companion:** Ada Wong (composure, elegance, agenda), Faye Valentine (weaponized attractiveness, hidden depth)
**Soundtrack DNA:** ABBA, Europop, original compositions in the same register
**World aesthetic:** Mediterranean noir with supernatural infrastructure
**Social commentary:** Never stated. Always present. The player does the work.
**What it is NOT:** James Bond. Cesare doesn’t infiltrate. He arrives.

## THE CHARACTERS

-----

### CESARE — Protagonist

**One line:** A gay man in a perfect white suit dismantles a vampire mafia in a Mediterranean city to Europop. He is very good at this. The game does not explain why it finds this surprising.

**Background:**
Italian. Vaguely — the game doesn’t over-specify. Late 20s apparently, actually late 20s. Grew up in the world most people live in with no knowledge of what runs beneath it. Encountered the vampire aristocracy under circumstances that should have killed him. Didn’t. Has been working through this ever since in his own way, which involves a great deal of shooting and remarkably few conversations about feelings.

**Personality:**

- Confident without being arrogant — the distinction matters enormously
- Wit as default mode, not performance. He roasts people. Enemies, allies, occasionally the architecture
- Never brooding. Never explaining himself
- Default expression: mild amusement, faint smirk, eyes that suggest he already knows the punchline
- Genuinely funny. The game is genuinely funny when he’s talking
- The flirting with attractive male enemies is not played for laughs — it’s completely natural and they find it deeply unsettling
- Has processed grief in his own way and that way involves not discussing it and shooting vampires

**His sexuality — design approach:**
Never announced. Never hidden. Present from the beginning in ways that are natural and easy to miss if you’re not paying attention. The straight player who has identified with him doesn’t get a reveal — they get a gradual understanding. By the time it’s completely unambiguous they’ve been playing as him for hours and attachment has already happened.

The progression is deliberate:

- **Early game:** Glances. Half second pauses on certain enemies. Ambiguous wit. The player who’s looking sees it immediately
- **Mid game:** Clearer. A comment that can’t really be read any other way. Vera’s exasperation
- **Late game:** Completely unambiguous. The scugnizzo moment. He sees someone attractive and simply notices, openly, without drama
- **Throughout:** The impossibly muscular enemies that Japanese games are inexplicably full of receive approximately thirty percent more attention than tactically necessary

The player who finishes Boynetta realizing they spent fifteen hours inhabiting a gay man and had the time of their life — that’s the point. That’s the commentary that never announces itself.

**Physical:**

- Mediterranean Italian, late 20s, dark hair — started perfect, the universe happened to it
- Athletic and buff — the suit fits because he actually looks like that. The build reads almost normal until the jacket catches the wind
- Gold necklace, always present, clearly significant, never explained in game one
- White suit (day), black suit (night) — changes more than once, treated as completely normal
- Leather shoulder holster under the shirt, always. The guns were always there
- The suit does a split. The universe accepts this

**What he is NOT:**

- Not a Bayonetta reskin
- Not defined by being gay — he simply is
- Not camp as mockery — camp as complete sincerity
- Not brooding — he has feelings that occasionally surface briefly before being redirected with a perfectly timed joke

### VERA — Companion

*Name: VERA or MARA — both work across European languages without belonging to any specific one.*

**One line:** A French woman who looks 28, is approximately 200, knows considerably more than she mentions, and is frequently mistaken for Cesare’s love interest. She finds this mildly useful.

**Background:**
French. Born sometime in the early 19th century. Has been navigating the vampire aristocracy’s world since before most current institutions existed. Found Cesare specifically — their meeting was not accidental on her part. He does not know this yet. She knew the boyfriend. She knew what the necklace was before it was given to Cesare. She is waiting for him to ask the right question. He has not asked the right question.

**Personality:**

- Composed. Not cold — composed. The distinction matters
- Patience that comes from watching humans make the same mistakes for two centuries
- Can tell Cesare things he doesn’t want to hear and have him actually listen
- Laughs at his jokes — not all of them, which makes the ones she laughs at mean something
- Mildly exasperated by his flirting in the way you’re exasperated at a friend you’ve known for years: *“Can we move on? Can you concentrate without thinking of boys for an hour?”*
- Genuinely his friend. No betrayal arc. No hidden agenda against him
- Not playable — her power comes from a certain remove. She observes, she knows, she provides. Keeping her non-playable protects both her mystery and the game’s identity

**The couple assumption:**
Everyone makes it. They have developed a routine. Cesare has a different devastating response every time. Vera has a look that makes the asker feel briefly foolish. They find it genuinely funny. The game never addresses the subversion directly. It simply never happens.

**Her role:**
Between missions: merchant, information broker, upgrade dealer. She sells upgrades with the energy of someone recommending a wine pairing. Her shop is somewhere beautiful. She is always in the middle of knowing something when Cesare arrives, glasses in hand.

During missions: present, occasionally shoots someone with complete precision, provides the deadpan counterpoint to everything Cesare does.

**Physical:**

- Apparent age late 20s. Actual age: stopped counting sometime in the mid-1800s
- French — her hair reflects this. Perfect dark bob, straight, precise. She decided it would look exactly like this and it complied
- Red jacket with gold detail — stylish, immediately readable as dangerous
- Layered gold necklaces — accumulated over a very long time, all mean something
- Rings on her shooting hand. She is very precise. The rings are not a problem
- Glasses she holds rather than wears when thinking
- Looks exactly like what a lesser game would make the eye candy. Is not the eye candy. The game knows this and never explains it

**Her leitmotif:**
French chanson or something classical that has been gradually remixed across eras. A song that has been through several centuries because she has. You hear the bones of it early — stripped back, almost melancholic. By the end it’s something completely different but recognizable. Like she is.

**What she is NOT:**

- Not the evil twist
- Not the love interest
- Not cold — composed
- Not defined by her mystery — she is a complete person who also happens to have mysteries

## THE WORLD

**Setting:** Monreale — a fictional Mediterranean city with Sicilian soul. The overlap between several places that exist and one that doesn’t quite. Wealthy, ancient, beautiful on the surface. Present day, roughly.

*モンレアーレ in katakana. Platinum Games would absolutely use this name. This is intentional.*

The city looks like different eras collapsed into each other — Arab, Norman, Spanish, each leaving architecture on top of the previous layer. Which is exactly what a city secretly run by 400 year old vampires would look like. The vampire history is woven into the urban fabric. That palazzo exists because a vampire built it in 1340 and has never sold it.

**The double layer:**
The city’s organized crime has run European power structures for centuries because the people at the top cannot die. The mafia IS the vampire aristocracy. They have been feeding on the city — economically, politically, literally — for longer than anyone currently alive can remember.

The social commentary is present. It is never stated aloud.

**The vampire aristocracy:**

- Ancient, formal, obsessed with hierarchy and decorum
- Find Cesare personally offensive — he represents chaos, visibility, refusal to accept inherited power
- The contrast between their calcified formality and his energy IS the game’s visual language
- They know Vera. Not as prey. A 400 year old lieutenant will choose his words carefully around her
- They have galas. They maintain legitimate social infrastructure alongside the violence. Of course they do. They’ve had centuries to build cover

## COMBAT

**Cesare’s weapons:** Guns (distance, contempt) and swords (close, theatrical, deliberate)

The guns were always there — leather shoulder holster under the shirt the entire time. The suit wasn’t hiding who he is. It was just the outermost layer of someone always prepared.

The transition between weapons is the combat art. It should feel like a performance moving between acts.

**The feel:**

- Combat as choreography — it looks like dancing, specifically the kind that ends with someone unconscious
- Where Bayonetta is feline, Cesare is a stage performer who happens to be lethal
- The double jump exists. No explanation is offered. The game moves on

**Vera shoots:**
When necessary. Which is different from always. She is very precise. The rings are not a problem.

**The Witch Time equivalent:**
Perfect dodge triggers slow motion. The Europop drops to half tempo. Snaps back to full BPM when he smirks. Not commented on by anyone.

**FLIRTING DURING COMBAT:**
Is a real tracked mechanic. Increases certain stats. The game considers it an achievement.

## THE SCORE SYSTEM

Cesare gets points and combos. This is simultaneously essential and pointless except for buying power-ups. This is part of the fun.

**The ranks, in ascending order:**
DISASTROUS / MEDIOCRE / SASSY / FLAMBOYANT / SUPER / ICONIC

**The end-chapter score screen:**
Appears behind the dialogue. Extremely complicated. More information than necessary.

BASE SCORE
ELEGANCE BONUS
STYLE MULTIPLIER
SUIT INTEGRITY BONUS
ENEMY VARIETY BONUS
TIME BONUS
UNNECESSARY FLIP: +800 *(always exactly 800. the wiki has seventeen theories. the studio will not confirm if intentional. it is intentional)*
FLIRTING DURING COMBAT BONUS
DAMAGE TAKEN PENALTY
SUIT DAMAGE PENALTY *(heavily weighted. the game takes this seriously)*
VERA DISAPPROVAL PENALTY *(real tracked stat. nobody fully understands the calculation)*

A medal spins in the corner with slightly too much visual flourish for what it is.

**SUIT INTEGRITY** is the most controversial mechanic. Advanced players dodge everything perfectly not for survival but because suit damage feels personally offensive. The community speedrun category is called IMMACULATE SUIT.

**The end-chapter dialogue by rank:**

DISASTROUS:
Vera: “…”
Cesare: “I know.”
Vera: “Do you though.”
Cesare: “…yes.”

MEDIOCRE:
Vera: “That was uninspired.”
Cesare: “They were boring enemies.”
Vera: “They were the same enemies as always.”
Cesare: “Exactly.”

SASSY:
Vera: *looks up from whatever she’s reading*
Vera: “Sassy.”
Cesare: “It’s a valid rank.”
Vera: *goes back to reading*

FLAMBOYANT:
Vera: “Better.”
Cesare: “Thank you.”
Vera: “That wasn’t a compliment.”
Cesare: “I know. I’m taking it anyway.”

SUPER:
Vera: “Super. Really.”
Cesare: “I think I was, why? You got notes?”
Vera: “Several.”
Cesare: “…”
Cesare: “Were any of them good notes?”
Vera: *almost smiles*

ICONIC:
Vera says nothing.
Cesare waits.
Vera: “…don’t make it weird.”
Cesare makes it weird.

## THE SOUNDTRACK

Europop. Committed, unironic, inseparable from his identity.

The music slowing during dramatic moments and snapping back to full BPM when he smirks IS the emotional language of the game. The tempo IS the narrative punctuation.

**His signature song** — the equivalent of Fly Me To The Moon — original, written for the game. Europop DNA. Works at half tempo for drama and full tempo for everything else. Plays when he does something truly unreasonable.

**Vera’s leitmotif:** French chanson or classical, gradually remixed across centuries. Heard early as something stripped and melancholic. Unrecognizable by the end until suddenly it isn’t.

-----

## THE AESTHETIC — EUROPE THROUGH JAPANESE EYES

The visual language is deliberate almost-rightness:

**Animations:** Hair and fabric with exactly that specific Japanese game physics — slightly too dramatic, slightly too slow. Clearly studied and replicated the feel rather than reality. The jacket does the thing. The suit does the thing.

**The HUD:** Overly complicated. More information than needed in fonts slightly too ornate. A style meter with a Japanese-adjacent name that doesn’t quite translate. Numbers appearing during combat for reasons that take a moment to parse.

**The fonts:** Almost right. European type foundry chosen by someone who played too much DMC. Recognizably inspired. Distinctly its own.

**The menus:** Logical once you learn the logic. The logic is not immediately obvious.

**The loading screens:** Unnecessarily dramatic.

**The names:** チェーザレ. モンレアーレ. Pronounced by Japanese voice actors with complete sincerity. Italians smile. This is intentional. This is the whole joke executed with complete commitment.

## THE TORN SUIT SCENE

Cesare takes significant damage mid-game. Enemies tear his white suit — shots, knife blows. He dodges, sacrificing the suit rather than his body. Expression: mild irritation at the damage to the jacket rather than fear.

He pauses.

Grabs the torn fabric. Tears off the remaining jacket deliberately, on his terms.

The camera pushes into close up of torn shirt revealing leather shoulder holster straps across his chest. Holds. Slow pan across arms and shoulders. Gold necklace catching the light. Slow rise back to face.

He glances down briefly.

Looks directly at camera.

Half smile.

Reaches for both guns simultaneously. Draws in one fluid motion.

Europop snaps back to full BPM.

He turns toward the enemies.

-----

Same framing as Bayonetta’s equivalent scene. Same commitment. Complete sincerity. The game does not wink. The player connects the dots. The audio is doing most of the work — committed as high drama, delivered as camp. The only correct approach.

## THE GALA SCENE

The vampire aristocracy has galas. Of course they do.

Cesare arrives. Black suit. Vera beside him, devastating. Every head turns. Everyone makes the wrong assumption. He is not there to infiltrate — he doesn’t infiltrate. He arrives. The vampires know immediately who he is. This is fine. He knows they know. Also fine.

The valet is young and attractive. Cesare hands over something with approximately thirty percent more eye contact than strictly necessary. A comment. The valet doesn’t know what to do with this.

Vera, already three steps ahead, without turning:
*“Can we—”*
*“Coming.”*
She almost smiles.

Later: he exits through a window — thrown or jumped, the game lets you decide — from approximately four floors up. Lands on the car being valeted. Car alarm. Beat. Gets up. Straightens the black suit. Checks the necklace. The suit is somehow fine. Looks at the valet.

Says something. We don’t hear it clearly.

The valet’s expression does something complicated.

Cesare runs.

Vera, already running, without looking back:
*“Are you DONE?”*
*“He gave me his number.”*
Three seconds of silence.
*“Of course he did.”*

## THE MYTHOLOGY: WHAT IS CESARE?

Not entirely human. Not a vampire. Something without a category, which alarms the vampire aristocracy more than anything else about him.

**What happened:**
The boyfriend was adjacent to the vampire world. An encounter went wrong — killed the boyfriend, should have killed Cesare. Vampire blood entered involuntarily. Not a bite. Something messier. Cesare survived impossibly. The necklace was on him. Whether it did something or simply survived with him is deliberately unclear.

**What he has now:**
Reflexes and aim beyond human capability. The double jump. A necklace that occasionally responds to things. No reliable answers about any of it. Vera has answers. He has not asked the right question.

**The ancient vampire boss, eventually:**
*“What ARE you?”*
Cesare straightens the necklace.
Does not answer.
The game moves on.

-----

## THE NECKLACE

The boyfriend’s gift. Gold. Always present. Vera knew what it was before it was given to him. This is one of the things she has not mentioned. The game knows what it is. The player finds out when the game decides. That information is withheld here deliberately.

-----

## THE EX-BOYFRIEND

Never seen. Never named for most of the game. Present everywhere — in what Cesare does not say, in a name that makes him pause half a second before shooting, in why Vera is here at all.

The reveal lands with weight because he has been so composed. The flamboyance and wit are genuine — but also how he moves through grief. Shown once, briefly, in a scene where the music stops entirely. Then starts again.

-----

## WHAT NEEDS TO EXIST NEXT

- Cesare’s signature song
- The vampire aristocracy’s structure — ranks, centuries of history, boss personalities
- What the necklace actually is *(the writer knows; revealed in game two if at all)*
- Vera’s full origin *(the writer knows; the player pieces it together)*
- The boyfriend’s name *(revealed late, once, deliberately)*
- Whether the title is Boynetta or something that nods to it without being it
- The city’s internal geography — which districts, which vampire family controls what

-----

## THE THEORETICAL CROSSOVER

Will never happen. Is perfect anyway.

She appears. He appears. One second of complete mutual recognition between two people who understand everything about each other immediately.

She turns back to back with him.
He adjusts his lapel.
She does the hair thing.
Both soundtracks play simultaneously. It works.
They start shooting in opposite directions.
Cut to title card.

No dialogue. None needed. Icons recognize icons.
